Level 1: Adulting & Building

Hello, Real World.

The era of learning how to be a 'real adult'—building real-world products through code and screaming my lungs out at the baseball stadium.

Building as a developer

I started dreaming of becoming a developer in high school. Right after I graduated, I joined a startup. My first company was 'Wash Swat', which runs a contactless laundry service.

There I worked as a workflow developer and built internal websites for many corners of the company—the factory, customer support, marketing, and more. Along the way I learned how software actually gets used, and how to talk with people on the ground.

My second company offered corporate M&A services. I joined as one of the very first members, so I got to wear many hats. The work I loved most was turning results into numbers, making small lean changes, shipping them right away, and watching performance grow. Because the team was small, I also did work that had nothing to do with engineering. When an M&A manager was away, I tried real brokerage—and although the deal was small, I got to own it through to closing.

Running a baseball community

After seventeen, my life was not only work. I skied, watched movies, traveled—but baseball was the thing I loved most. In the KBO, I cheer for the Doosan Bears. Not many of my friends were Bears fans, so I started a baseball community myself.

Fans of all ten KBO teams could join, and I aimed it at people in their twenties. Through nights at the stadium and weekend trips, I made a lot of friends. Today, about a hundred of us are in it together.

Level 2: Pivot & Freedom

Designing My Own Rules.

Stepping out of the safe code editor. Now carving my own path out on the snowy slopes and in the boundless world of business.

Why entrepreneurship

For three years I worked as a developer at startups and collected a wide range of real experience. I didn’t only write code—I also took on marketing, sales, and operations. That mix made me look at my strengths again.

Sitting still and focusing on code was not as natural for me as work where I could actually move the numbers, especially sales. I also started to think that, as AI grows, a strong foundation will help more than chasing every new tool. New technologies keep arriving, and AI learns faster than I do—but I am still the one who has to decide.

So I chose Entrepreneurship, a major where I can learn how to use management, startups, marketing, and sales in real life. I’m only a first-year, so I haven’t learned everything in the department yet. Lately I’ve been studying big data and marketing, and it has been powerful to stack that theory onto work I used to do without a textbook. I also love being in school—a place where it’s okay to be incomplete, and where I can keep challenging myself.

Skiing, a new season

Skiing is a sport I started last winter. With Korea’s heavy snow, it feels like a sport this country is built for. There are about ten ski resorts here, and I ski most often at Jisan Forest Resort. It’s about an hour from Seoul, and a little more affordable than many other mountains.

I went a lot with friends, and this time I joined Sookmyung Ski, a club under the university ski federation. It’s a training-camp style club—a good place to learn skiing more seriously. I can ski basic long turns now, and my goal this season is to take on carving.

Media English Assignment

Gold Medal, Negative Headlines

A critical review of how the media framed Korea’s fifth consecutive Asian Games baseball gold medal.

My overall opinion

Overall, I have a negative opinion of this article. Although most of its basic facts are correct, its tone is overly critical and its framing is unbalanced.

South Korea had won its fifth consecutive Asian Games gold medal less than a day before the article was published. However, the article focused mainly on how Korean professionals struggled against Japanese amateurs. I think this timing and emphasis were inappropriate because they reduced the significance of the players’ achievement.

Moreover, Japan’s players were not ordinary recreational amateurs. Many of them belonged to competitive corporate baseball teams and trained within Japan’s highly developed baseball system. Therefore, Korea’s 3–1 victory should not be treated as unimpressive simply because the Japanese players were officially classified as amateurs.

01

Newsworthiness and timing

The story is newsworthy because it concerns a Korea–Japan final, an international sporting event, and Korea’s fifth consecutive gold medal. It was timely in a journalistic sense, but its negative tone was inappropriate so soon after the victory.

02

English used

The article uses formal reporting verbs such as reported, noted, described, and highlighted. Its spelling and grammar are generally correct, but some sentences sound translated or exaggerated.

03

The headline

The headline is eye-catching and related to the story, but it is also sensational. The repeated contrast between “Korean professionals” and “Japanese amateurs” encourages readers to judge Korea negatively before reading the full article.

04

The lead paragraph

The lead clearly presents the score, the gold medal, and the main issue. However, it gives more attention to Korea’s struggle than to the achievement of winning the championship.

05

The 5 W’s

The article includes who, what, when, where, why, and how. However, it does not sufficiently explain that Japan’s corporate baseball players are highly trained elite athletes rather than ordinary amateurs.

06

Quotations and balance

It includes reports from TBS, Mainichi Shimbun, NNN, and The Digest, as well as a direct quotation from Park Young-hyun. However, it lacks comments from Korea’s manager, independent experts, and Japanese players.

07

Article length

The article is not extremely long, but it feels repetitive because several Japanese media outlets make almost the same point. Repeated photo captions and unrelated recommendations also interrupt the flow.

08

Significance and human interest

The article has significance because it discusses an international final, a major rivalry, and the competitiveness of Korean baseball. Park Young-hyun’s comments about helping his teammates also add human interest.

09

Quality as a news report

The article is timely, detailed, and easy to follow. However, its sensational headline, limited context, and lack of balanced opinions prevent it from being a fully objective sports report.

10

Real or fake?

The article is real. The result and the tournament can be confirmed through official records and independent news reports. However, a real article can still contain biased framing, exaggeration, or misleading wording.

Final verdict: Real news, but not completely balanced news. The facts are mostly reliable, while the headline and selection of details make Korea’s victory sound more negative than necessary.
