Dear Mr. Dempsey,

Re: Denied boarding on Flight F9 1993, November 2, 2026

I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with how Frontier Airlines handled the denied boarding of my mother and me. I flew with Frontier often while living in the United States, so I was particularly disappointed by this experience.

We had purchased two one-way tickets for $301 on Flight F9 1993 from Denver to Las Vegas, departing at 10:30 AM. We had checked in online and received boarding passes for seats 18A and 18B. We arrived at the gate on time, but the gate agent told us that the flight was overbooked and that our seats had been given to other passengers. We did not voluntarily give up our seats. The ticket counter was unattended at first, and after waiting about 90 minutes, we were told to call customer service. There, the agent asked us to pay an additional $198 for rebooking.

Because our hotel in Las Vegas was already booked, we could not postpone our trip. We declined to pay the $198 rebooking fee and instead purchased replacement tickets at our own expense, costing $395. It is unreasonable that we should bear this cost because of Frontier’s overbooking.

I therefore request a refund of our original $301 tickets, reimbursement of the $395 we paid for replacement tickets, and the compensation required under U.S. Department of Transportation regulations. I would appreciate your response within 14 days of the date of this letter. If the matter is not resolved within this period, I will file a complaint with the DOT, and I will regretfully stop flying with Frontier.

Thank you in advance for your prompt attention to this matter.

Yours sincerely,

Eunji Lee

Eunji Lee

Enclosures: Expedia booking confirmation, boarding passes, receipt for replacement tickets

My Thoughts

Reflection

I haven’t had anything to complain about recently, so I made up a situation for this letter. I chose flight overbooking because it happens more often than I expected. While writing the letter, I learned how to explain the problem clearly, include details like dates and costs, and ask for a specific solution politely but firmly. If I ever have a similar experience in the future, I think I can handle it in this way.
